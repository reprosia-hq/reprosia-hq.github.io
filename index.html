    Agents can write the code. The record is still missing.

    Four questions come up whenever an auditor looks at AI-assisted analysis. Each one maps to something that exists today or something being built.

    • What did the AI touch?

      regulog writes each action to a hash-chained, user-attributed log, so a missing or altered entry shows up on verification. Capturing prompts and model output in that same log is in development.

      regulog on CRAN
      AI capture in development

    • Which data was it run on?

      lineager traces rows from source to analysis dataset, records every exclusion with its reason, and writes a provenance report styled after the CDISC Reviewer's Guide.

      lineager on CRAN

    • Who reviewed it, and what did they decide?

      Most AI workflows keep no record of who accepted, edited or rejected an output. This is the first thing Reprosia is adding on top of regulog.

      In development

    • Can someone re-run it and get the same result?

      reproducr captures the session, locks dependencies, certifies scripts and flags drift when results change.

      reproducr on CRAN

    Four packages carry the evidence.

    They are open source, maintained by the same author, and usable without Reprosia.

    PackageWhat it doesStatus
    regulogTamper-evident, hash-chained audit logging designed around 21 CFR Part 11 and EU Annex 11. Ships with IQ/OQ/PQ qualification scripts.On CRAN

    Regulators are asking for this kind of record.

    In January 2026 the FDA and EMA published joint guiding principles for AI in drug development. They are non-binding, and they point to documented data provenance, controlled and reproducible environments, and records of human review.

    The approach page goes through the ten principles one by one and says how far Reprosia gets you on each, including where it does not help.

    Our reading draws on a third-party summary. Check the official text before you rely on any wording.
