Where the records get used.

Five situations where a statistician, QA lead or regulatory reviewer needs evidence, and what produces it.

Reviewing AI-drafted analysis code

In development

Who
Statistical programmers and QC reviewers.
The problem
A draft from an assistant is merged with no record of the prompt, the model or who approved it.
Evidence produced
A log entry chain holding the prompt, output, reviewer decision and data hash.

Tracing populations through an analysis

On CRAN

Who
Study statisticians.
The problem
The sample size changes between datasets and nobody can say exactly which rows were dropped, or why.
Evidence produced
A lineager provenance report listing every exclusion and its reason.

Documenting a Bayesian prior

On CRAN

Who
Trial statisticians and regulatory affairs.
The problem
An informative prior needs a recorded source, a check against the data, and a sensitivity analysis.
Evidence produced
A bayprior report covering elicitation, conflict diagnostics and sensitivity analysis.

Qualifying an R environment

On CRAN

Who
QA and IT validation teams.
The problem
You need evidence that a package is installed and behaves as specified.
Evidence produced
IQ/OQ/PQ scripts and results for regulog, with each operational test traced to a 21 CFR Part 11 or EU Annex 11 clause.

Showing that SAS and R agree

Consulting

Who
Teams moving programmes from SAS to R.
The problem
Reviewers want proof that migrated outputs match the originals.
Evidence produced
Side-by-side comparison documentation and a migration audit trail.
